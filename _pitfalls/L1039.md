---
layout: pitfall
title: "Unary minus binds looser than the power operator"
permalink: /p/L1039/
description: >-
  In Lua `^` has higher precedence than unary minus and is right-associative,
  so `-2 ^ 2` is `-(2 ^ 2) = -4` and `2 ^ 3 ^ 2` is `2 ^ (3 ^ 2) = 512`. Since
  Lua 5.3 `^` also always produces a float, even for integer operands.
runtime:
  - Lua 5.1
  - Lua 5.2
  - Lua 5.3
  - Lua 5.4
  - Lua 5.5
  - LuaJIT
---

```lua
print(-2 ^ 2)             -- -4.0: -(2^2), not (-2)^2
print(2 ^ 3 ^ 2)          -- 512.0: right-associative, 2^(3^2)
print(2 ^ 3)              -- 8.0: always a float on Lua 5.3+
print(math.type(2 ^ 3))   -- float
```

Use parentheses when you mean a negative base, and remember that `^` never
returns an integer on Lua 5.3+:

```lua
print((-2) ^ 2)   -- 4.0
print(2 ^ -1)     -- 0.5

local square = 3 * 3        -- 9 (integer), not 3 ^ 2
```
